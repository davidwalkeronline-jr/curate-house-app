Our Vision

Pursuing God's Heart for the Restoration of All Things

Grace Monroe was planted in 2010 with a big vision for a small town: to help people encounter Jesus, discover, and live out their God-given identity in ways that transform their community and impact the world. From its roots in an abandoned cotton mill, Grace Monroe has blossomed into a church known for biblical teaching, authentic discipleship, and vibrant worship.

In 2018, as we outgrew the Mill, the Lord led us to the abandoned Monroe school property on Bold Springs Avenue, sitting empty and vandalized at the entrance to our town. Even then, we knew this campus was meant to be a blessing to the city: a hub for mission and ministry where life happens all week long.

For 16 years, Grace Monroe has lived into the vision that first brought this community together. By God's grace, we have seen lives transformed, families restored, dreams awakened, leaders empowered, and a city stirred.

Why this matters now

The challenge isn't a lack of vision. It's a lack of capacity.

01

We're running out of space

For the first time in our history we've had to cap KidzLife. Students have no room to grow, and there is no space left for discipleship classes, groups, or our ministry team.

02

A 120-year-old campus has real needs

Our historic school building is packed with potential, and it also carries aging infrastructure, including significant stormwater issues, that need attention.

03

A vision not yet fully lived

Community partners are ready to go deeper with us. We sense a fuller calling to raise up leaders, awaken Kingdom dreams, and serve as a hub for mission throughout Monroe.

This moment is not ultimately about buildings. It is about removing barriers to disciple-making, leadership development, and community impact. If we do nothing, growth stalls. If we act wisely, mission can multiply.

The opportunity before us

Step by Step, Debt Free

Through Acorns to Oaks, we turned the first three buildings of this 8.5-acre campus into our church home. This next phase activates two of the three remaining empty buildings, adds GraceKidz classrooms, creates outdoor gathering space, and addresses infrastructure. Each step begins once it is fully funded.

Step 1$1.5M

Gathering Space & Infrastructure

Renovate the former cafeteria into a large group space with a church kitchen, repair stormwater issues, and add an outdoor gathering area for students and groups.

Step 2$500K

GraceKidz Expansion

Turn current office space into new GraceKidz classrooms and a dedicated sensory room for families with special needs, removing the cap on children's ministry.

Step 3$2.0M

Midland Ministry Hub

A new home for church offices, group and training rooms, an expanded counseling center, space for ministry partners, and our Leadership Residency.

Step 4$500K

Site Work & Parking

Expand parking along Midland Ave. and complete site and accessibility improvements so the campus stays safe as it grows.

Step 5$500K

Community Commercial Kitchen

A licensed kitchen for local food entrepreneurs and our farmer's market partners, helping address food insecurity in a USDA-designated food desert.

Retire our existing campus mortgage so that within five years Grace Monroe carries zero debt into its next season of ministry.

Frequently Asked Questions

The plan

How does Bold Springs relate to Acorns to Oaks?+

Acorns to Oaks funded the first phase on our campus: the worship auditorium, student center, children's classrooms, coffee shop, preschool, and courtyard. Bold Springs is the next chapter, activating two of the three remaining buildings on this 8.5-acre campus.

Where did the $6 million goal come from?+

Each of the six steps was scoped and priced individually, based on our current financial baseline and initial architect and contractor input. The total reflects those real numbers rather than a round target.

Why debt free, and what about our current debt?+

We currently carry about $1.36 million from previous construction: the 2020 campus mortgage (now about $1,045,000) and a second loan for the student gym. Payments on both are fully covered by lease income from campus partners. Rather than adding new debt, we're asking the Lord to provide the full $6 million so we can complete all six steps and retire our debt entirely.

What about more room for Sunday worship?+

Denton Hall seats 320 on a typical Sunday and feels comfortably full around 308. We average about 200 per service, so there is still room to grow. When we near capacity, our first step will be a third service before considering an auditorium expansion.

Leadership & timeline

Who has oversight of this campaign?+

The campaign and renovation remain under Grace Monroe's own leadership. No separate nonprofit is being used. Our Elders approve and oversee the provisional goals and validate key milestones. A design team of staff, board members, architects, and engineers is leading the planning.

What is the timeline for construction?+

Because we're building debt free, each step begins once it is fully funded. The pace depends, in part, on how the Lord provides through this campaign. We'll share more specific timelines as commitments come in.

Your response

How can I know what to give?+

Many of us feel more fear than faith when it comes to finances. This is an opportunity to ask God for faith to trust Him with our whole lives, including our resources. Start with the open-handed prayer: "God, out of what You've placed in my hands, what would You have me give over and above my regular church giving to this project?"

Should I reduce my regular giving?+

Please don't. Our week-to-week ministry depends on the faithful tithes and offerings of our congregation. Bold Springs is "over and above" giving, in addition to, not instead of, your regular giving.

Why make a commitment instead of giving as we go?+

Knowing what households plan to give over the two-year period lets us plan responsibly and build debt free, sequencing each step as funding comes in rather than borrowing against the future.

When do I respond with my commitment?+

Commitment Sunday is November 15. During our gathering you'll have the opportunity to bring your commitment card forward as an act of worship. Commitments are recorded confidentially by our Finance team.

Can I give something other than cash or checks?+

Yes. Gifts can come from appreciated stock, mutual funds, donor-advised funds, and IRA qualified charitable distributions for those 70½ or older. Appreciated assets can sometimes offer added tax advantages. Contact our Finance team at monroe@gfc.tv and we'll help with the details.

How should I complete my gift? Are there tax benefits?+

The giving period runs November 2026 through December 2028, spanning three tax years: 2026, 2027, and 2028. A pledge can be fulfilled across three tax-filing years, which may allow for a larger gift than a single year would. Give weekly, monthly, quarterly, annually, or as a one-time gift.

If things change

What if we fall short of our goal?+

Each step activates only once its funding is in hand. If we fall short, we will adjust our timeline, not the vision, moving through the six steps in order as the Lord provides rather than taking on debt.

What if we raise more than $6 million?+

That would be wonderful! Gifts beyond the goal would let us move sooner into future phases of the campus. There is still untapped potential here, including a 27,000 sq. ft. classroom building and a future parking area.
